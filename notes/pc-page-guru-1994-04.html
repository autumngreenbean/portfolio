    It's like a scene from a painting of hell. What does anyone think they're showing off by photographing the bound bodies of women? "Human beings are sorrowful creatures. The women, seemingly without will and with their gazes hidden, lie sprawled and sway limply as subjects of the photographs. Is this what masochism is? These women neither beg forgiveness for unreasonable punishment or pain nor flinch in fear. By tacit agreement they accept the rope and put their own bodies on display. The men have handed their gaze over to the camera and have even stopped looking with their own eyes. Each person's heart and soul remain hidden somewhere. Even so, a strange, shared tension builds, a game that stays on the surface, like the clear liquid floating on top. The distorted bodies on screen are photographs, and by being presented in the virtual space inside the box of a display, they have been thoroughly filtered. There's no body odor, no moaning, no pain, no fear, only the shapes that result from the binding. Does the electronic order of the CD-ROM medium drain the world that felt "like a painting of hell" this thoroughly of its substance, or does it purify it and invite us into a new world of beauty? To my eye, it's drained. The bound women, the men taking the photographs, everyone has been drained. They all seem to be playing parts in a game of make-believe.     This essay criticizes the term 'multimedia' used by competing corporate industries, citing it "is not a market, it is a technology". Page 54 ... to page 57 demonstrates how to make a title with Expanded Book, a Mac-only software used for making book layouts.

    Section 1: ノリだけで突っ走っていいのか? ("Is it fine to just charge ahead on vibes alone?")

    "Is it fine to just charge ahead on vibes alone?"

    そもそも、マルチメディアなんかに手を出すのは、世界を広げたいからでしょう?

    "In the first place, the reason you get involved in something like multimedia is that you want to broaden your world, isn't it?"

    Section 4: マルチメディアは市場でなく技術である ("Multimedia is a technology, not a market")
    マルチメディア「技術」をどう応用するかはユーザーの自由だ。メーカーやソフトハウスは素材を提供するだけ。その素材を、System 7やWindows 3.1やUNIXの上でどのように組み合わせて使うかは、ユーザーに任されている。

    "How to apply multimedia 'technology' is the user's freedom. Makers and software houses merely supply the materials. How those materials are combined and used on System 7, Windows 3.1, or UNIX is left to the user."

    さて皆さん、マルチメディアは、だいたいこんな感じの世界であります。マルチメディア・タイトルを作るというのもマルチメディアですが、マルチメディア＝マルチメディア・タイトルというワケではないワケです。この世界の中で、あなたはどのマルチメディアをやりますか?

    "Well then, everyone, multimedia is more or less a world like this. Making a multimedia title is also multimedia, but it is not the case that multimedia equals multimedia titles. Within this world, which multimedia will you do?"

    Everyone has a habit or two they just can’t cure. Some people may seem to have no such habits at all, but really they just don’t show up around other people, and who knows what they do when they’re completely alone. Then again, nobody can point out habits like that, and the person may not even notice them. Habits are a perfect way to understand a person. People say you should cure bad habits, but if curing one meant you could no longer tell who that person was, maybe there’s no need to be so insistent about it. For one thing, the person doesn’t feel the habit is bad. So even I never thought of curing mine. My habit is whistling. If I’m walking alone, I start whistling before I know it. I keep whistling on and on, a dubious melody nobody has ever heard, in a sloppy, made-up phrasing. When I was in junior high, my mother caught me coming home whistling and scolded me, “You shouldn’t whistle at night.” But this one habit still won’t go away, even now that I’m thirty. That day too, I apparently was whistling as I walked [along the underground passage in Shinjuku Station toward the subway platforms]. It must have been the usual off-the-cuff, careless tune. Suddenly I noticed a tall man in glasses and a black fedora walking right next to me. And this suspicious-looking man kept glancing at me.

    「Excuse me.」 Suddenly the man bowed his head to me and said this. He was peering at me with creepy eyes like bottomless pits. 「You were whistling just now, weren’t you?」 「What about it?」 「I was impressed by the wonderful tone. No, I’m not a suspicious person, really.」 What on earth was this man, who looked like a cheap scarecrow? I thought I had no time to get involved with some unknown character. 「I don’t have time.」 As I said this and started to leave, the man pressed his mouth right against my ear and whispered: 「You could make a bit of money.」 「A bit of money,」 he said, 「with that whistling.」 This guy comes out of nowhere to talk to a total stranger and then tries to egg me on to make money. Is he out of his mind? Making money by whistling is ridiculous beyond belief. I shook him off and kept walking quickly through the underground passage. When I reached the subway ticket office and looked back, the man was gone. But after I got back to my apartment, the bespectacled man’s words suddenly began to nag at me. As I soaked in the bathtub up to my neck with my eyes closed, the man’s voice saying 「a bit of money」 began to circle around in my head, and eventually coiled up like a snake and stopped moving. The truth is that I had lost my previous job for a certain reason and had just started at a new company after a great deal of trouble finding one. But that new workplace was more uncomfortable than I expected and also boring, and after one week I was sick of it. To be blunt, I’m simply not cut out for office work. Still, I’m not clever enough to live off women or to gamble.
    I had resigned myself to just carrying on like [unclear], grumbling all the while, so the phrase 「a bit of money」 was truly an enticing phrase to me. One week later, at the same time, I was walking through the underground passage in Shinjuku, deliberately whistling loudly. Needless to say, I was hoping that if that man came again he would surely spot me and speak to me. Sure enough, the man appeared. He came up to me delightedly, like a dog wagging its tail. What followed was a series of astonishing events that you’d have trouble believing, and my life changed completely because of that day. To put it simply, in India there’s that street performance where a cobra raises its head from a basket when a flute is played, right? It’s the same thing. They bring a man with erectile dysfunction to me, he takes off his clothes and lies on his back, and I whistle. Something like pii-hyara pii-hyoro, an improvised melody. They get erect right away. You think I’m lying? I thought it was a lie at first too. In time with the pii-hyara, what had been hanging limp as if dead lifts its head. At first it wobbles, but before long it rises up firmly, standing like a cobra. One old man even ejaculated for the first time in thirty years from the whistling alone. He clung to me in tears and thanked me, saying he could die happy now. My fee per whistling session? I’ll leave that to your imagination.
